# Anti-plan-maxing: fiddle-until-right over heavy upfront planning

**Status:** Idea — not committed. Mined from "LIVE: Uncle Bob on Software Fundamentals in the Age of AI" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcLPGC-tvgk) by `cc-yt-idea-mine` on 2026-08-20.

## Premise

Bob tried plan-first with agents the very week of the interview and calls it "always a
disaster": plans are gorgeous, agents "run halfcocked" the moment reality diverges, and
you stop, rewrite, restart. His alternative is the agile move re-run at agent speed:
build a story or two, look at what came out, reorganize, repeat — because the cost of
change "has plummeted to as close to zero as I think we're ever going to get it."

## The bet

For cheap-to-change work, iterate-and-look beats plan-then-build. If true, the plan-first
center of gravity in this config is over-applied.

## Decisions / open questions

- **This idea Contradicts the planner/builder protocol** (Fable plans, Opus builds from a
  plan file) **and the plan-first skills** (`/explore-plan`, `superpowers:writing-plans`,
  kickoff's phased plan). The honest counter: the protocol exists partly for model-cost
  routing, not just sequencing — so the right outcome may be scoping *when* plan-first
  applies (irreversible/expensive work) rather than deleting it. The protocol's own
  "split planner from builder only when build ≫ plan" clause is the seed of the same
  insight. This capture forces the call; it does not pre-decide it.
- If adopted even partially: which skills get an "iterate mode," and what marks a task as
  cheap-to-change?

## Credible first step

Write the boundary as one CLAUDE.md paragraph — "plan-first when X, iterate-first when Y"
— and trial it for two weeks of real sessions before touching any skill.
